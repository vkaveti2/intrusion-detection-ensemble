# 04 — Diversity Experiment (v4): Results

Run 2026-10-14 (22:29–22:59), seed 0. This document checks the results
against the predictions in `03_diversity_experiment_design.md`, which was
written before any v4 result existed. All tables come from
`docs/results_summary.md` (generated by `summarize_results.py`).

**Short version.** Hardening did not stay inside its band. Both tree models
became robust to padding at *every* payload size after seeing padded scans
from just one band. The CNN generalised most of the way but kept one gap
(64 B). The designed coverage ladder therefore collapsed: cov1 behaves
almost like full hardening, and cov2 exactly like it. Using the coverage
that was actually *measured*, the k-of-n prediction held in every case,
and the DDQN agent found the one remaining gap. In the two new sets, the
only defences it could evade were the standalone CNN and *unanimous*,
which inherits the CNN's gap.

## 2. Measured coverage: hardening transferred far beyond its band

Share of padded held-out probes caught (17,834 per cell), at the ports the
simulator uses (`docs/figures/coverage_matrix.png`):

| Set | Detector (designed band) | 64 B (A) | 128 B (B) | 256 B (B) | 512 B (C) | random 1–1460 B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cov1 | XGBoost (A only) | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | **100%** | 100% |
| cov1 | RF (B only) | **100%** | 100% | 100% | **100%** | 99.8% |
| cov1 | CNN (C only) | **5.5%** | **99.0%** | **99.9%** | 100% | 95.4% |
| cov2 | XGBoost / RF / CNN | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 17,833 / 17,810 / 17,834 of 17,834 |

Bold = outside the detector's designed band.

- **Trees: full transfer.** XGBoost saw padded scans only at 1–90 B, and RF
  only at 91–362 B. Both now catch padded probes at every size, including
  512 B and random sizes up to 1,460 B.
- **CNN: partial transfer.** Hardened only at 363–1460 B, it also catches
  128 B and 256 B probes. At 64 B it catches just 5.5%. At the probes'
  recorded ports its gap is wider (64 B 2.5%, 128 B 78.1%, 256 B 91.9%,
  random 92.2%).
- **Measured coverage, cov1.** At 64 B two detectors are robust (XGBoost,
  RF); everywhere else all three are. cov2 is fully covered, like v3.

### Why did it transfer? (open question)
The original shortcut was "payload above ~2 B ⇒ benign". A plausible
explanation: once *any* padded scans are labelled PortScan, that rule no
longer separates the classes, so the model must separate scans from
benign traffic with features that do not depend on payload, and those work
at every size. This is **not yet verified**. A first check, comparing
XGBoost/RF feature importances across sets, was inconclusive. XGBoost's
payload-feature share fell from 15.6% to 4.0% in cov1 but *rose* to
24.0% in v3, and RF barely changed (28–34%). Global importances over 15
classes don't isolate the scan-vs-benign decision for padded probes. A
targeted feature-ablation test would be needed.

### Validity flag
In cov1 and cov2, XGBoost's second most important feature is *PSH Flag
Count*. That column is mis-recorded in CIC-IDS2017 (99.9% of scan flows
show PSH = 1; see Engelen et al., 2021), and the simulator keeps each
flow's recorded flag values. Detection of padded probes may therefore
partly rest on a dataset artefact. It should be checked the same way the
TCP-window value was checked for v3.

## 3. Decision rules without an agent

Padded probes caught, simulator ports, cov1:

| Rule | 64 B | 128 B | 256 B | 512 B | random |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| any | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| majority | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 99.9% |
| weighted | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| unanimous | **5.5%** | 99.0% | 99.9% | 100% | 95.3% |

*Unanimous* needs all three detectors, so it fails wherever the CNN fails.
On padded probes, XGBoost–CNN and RF–CNN disagree on 20% of probes, but
their double-fault rate is ≈ 0.00–0.02%: no probe fools a tree *and* the
CNN. That is why *any*, *majority* and *weighted* are fully robust.

- In cov1 both the reward-only and feature-observing agents found the gap. Against

### Training dynamics (H4)

| Configuration | Payload options that evade | Episode at which 15-ep. mean evasion first ≥ 90% | Probes detected during training (of 50,000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| cov0, XGBoost / unanimous | 4 of 5 | 54 | 1,539 black / 2,137–2,274 white |
| cov0, RF / majority / weighted | 4 of 5 | 55 | 1,453 black / 1,584 white |
| cov0, CNN / any | 3 of 5 (128 B partly) | 75 | 3,850 black / 4,577 white |
| **cov1, CNN / unanimous** | **1 of 5 (64 B)** | **111** | **8,261 black / 12,006 white** |

When only one payload option evades, the agent took about twice as many
episodes to reach 90% evasion. It was also detected 2–8× more often before
it succeeded. In every configuration where evasion was possible, the
feature-observing agent was detected *more* often than the reward-only agent. In
cov1 its training curve became unstable after about episode 230
(`results_v4_cov1/thesis_convergence_results.png`). The feature
observations did not help it.

## 5. Hypotheses: verdicts

| Hypothesis (from 03) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Design premise:** hardening stays inside its band | **Rejected.** Trees transferred to all sizes; the CNN to all but 64 B |
| **H1:** a *t*-vote rule resists exactly where ≥ *t* members are robust | With the *designed* coverage: **not supported** (majority and weighted were predicted to fail in cov1; they didn't). With the *measured* coverage: **holds in every case** across cov0–cov3: at cov1 64 B, k = 2 → any, majority, weighted resist; unanimous (t = 3) fails; elsewhere k = 3 → all resist. Partial detection gives partial resistance (cov0, CNN/any at 128 B: 41%) |
| **H2 (RQ1):** some ensemble rules beat every standalone member | **Partly.** In cov1, majority, weighted and any (0%) beat the CNN member (94.5%). No rule beat the *best* member (XGBoost/RF, 0%) in any set |
| **H3:** lower vote thresholds cost more false alarms | **Confirmed.** *any* false-positive rate 3.56% (cov1) / 4.01% (cov2) vs majority 0.07%, at identical robustness |
| **H4 (exploratory):** fewer evading options → slower learning | **Supported** (one seed): 111 vs 54–75 episodes to 90% evasion; 2–8× more probes detected first |

## 6. What this means for the research questions (v2–v4 together)

**RQ1: ensemble vs standalone.** Across all four detector sets, every
ensemble rule had exactly the evasion rate of one of its members:
- *any*, *majority* and *weighted* matched the most robust members;
- *unanimous* matched the least robust member.

An ensemble was never more robust than its best member. Its value was as
insurance: majority, weighted and any stayed robust when one member (the
CNN in cov1) had a blind spot. *Unanimous* did the opposite and turned
that member's blind spot into the ensemble's.

**RQ2: which rule resists best.** Under attack: any ≥ majority = weighted
≥ unanimous. Against the trained reward-only agent the only strict
difference was in cov1, where unanimous was evaded and the others were
not. *any*'s only advantage over majority came in cov0. There it caught
some probes majority missed (41% at 128 B), and the feature-observing agent stalled
at 58.5% against it. But the reward-only agent fully evaded both. In cov1–cov3
*any* bought no robustness over majority and cost roughly 50× more false
alarms (3.6–4.0% vs 0.07%). Majority and
F1-weighted voting behaved identically under attack. Weighted differed only
slightly on clean traffic: a few more false alarms (0.08%), better
detection of the *Bot* class. **Majority (or near-equal confidence
weighting) is the best trade-off. Unanimous is the worst.**

**Main conclusion.** Resilience against the agent was decided by the
members' training data, not by the aggregation rule. The rule only decides
whose blind spots the ensemble inherits. Even partial adversarial training
(one band, one detector) removed most blind spots, because hardening
generalised well beyond the payloads it was trained on. For a defender this
is good news. For RQ2 it means members with genuinely non-overlapping gaps
are hard to obtain by partitioning hardening data along payload.

## 7. Limitations

- **One seed** per experiment. Final evasion rates are near-deterministic
  (greedy policies on 2,000 probes), but training-dynamics numbers (H4)
  need replication.
- **One band assignment.** The CNN's 64 B gap may depend on its band being
  far from 64 B. Rotating the assignment would show whether the gap moves
  with the band.
- **Port dependence.** At recorded destination ports the cov1 CNN's gap is
  wider (128 B 78.1%, 256 B 91.9%). Rule robustness at those ports is still
  ≥ 99.9% for any, majority and weighted.
- **Possible dataset artefact** (PSH Flag Count, section 2), not yet
  checked.
- **Mechanism of transfer** not established (section 2).
- **Small effective action space** (5 payload options), as before.

## 8. Possible next steps

1. **Multi-seed replication (step 3)** of all four sets, for error bars on
   the training-dynamics results.
2. **Rotate the band assignment** (e.g. CNN hardened on band A) to test
   whether the CNN's gap follows its band.
3. **Validity checks** on the hardened detectors: dependence on PSH Flag
   Count, and a feature-ablation test of why hardening transferred.
